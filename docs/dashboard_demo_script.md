# Dashboard demo script (2–3 minutes at the stand)

Start: `python -m vascushift.cli pipeline` (once, ~30 s) then `python -m vascushift.cli demo --open-browser`
(or open `demo/index.html` directly). Works offline except the Chart.js CDN; the waveform panel needs the
local bundle (`demo/data/local/`, generated by the pipeline, never committed).

1. **Top of the page (15 s).** "Everything here is computed from real recordings by one command. Green
   badges are measured people, blue are published group means, brown is our model. No dataset links a
   face camera to the neck veins, so we validate the two layers separately and say so."
2. **Overview tiles (30 s).** Face camera heart rate within ~1.3 bpm on 42 people; the jugular vein loses
   ~63 % of its area per +0.5 g head-to-foot in every person; ISS crew: weightlessness is not the same as
   lying down (70 vs 81 mm²).
3. **Optical layer (45 s).** Method table → POS ≈ CHROM ≫ Green. Click a dot in "Every session" or open
   a prepared case:
   * `index.html?session=ubfc-d2-subject41` — standard POS drops to 50 bpm for 6 s; the experimental tracked
     estimate (brown dashed) stays on the finger reference.
   * `index.html?session=ubfc-d1-5-gt` — the hard case: head motion, every method fails; the quality score
     flags almost every window (honest failure, see Data quality).
   * `index.html?session=ubfc-d2-subject9` — a recording with a 0.75 s frame drop, handled by the frame clock.
   Faces are never shown — signals only (dataset consent).
4. **Physiology layer (45 s).** IJV area vs gz_body: individual tilt data (left), flight and ISS means (right).
   Toggle to IJV pressure. Point at gz_body = 0: lying flat (grey) vs ISS (aqua) differ. Partial-gravity
   flight values sit above the tilt curve — tilt alone cannot predict the Moon or Mars.
5. **Model bridge (20 s).** Move the slider: the compartment model with default parameters next to the real
   medians — "this is the part we model, not measure; fitting it is the next step."
6. **Data quality (15 s).** The problems we found (irregular frame rate, dropped frames, sensor dropouts,
   outlier pressures) and how the pipeline handles each.

Likely questions:
* *"Can the camera see the veins?"* — Not with public data: no dataset has neck video with a venous
  reference. We specify the recording that would (lateral neck camera + tilt/LBNP + ultrasound).
* *"Is 1.3 bpm good?"* — Published unsupervised baselines on UBFC report ~4–8 bpm under other protocols; our
  protocol differs (real frame clock, all 42 people, same estimator for reference and estimate), so we do
  not claim a leaderboard result.
* *"Where are the ESA flight data?"* — Not released (0 files in ESA HREDA, checked live by the pipeline).
